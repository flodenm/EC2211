### What do macroeconomists do?

1. **Measure and describe the economy**
  - Output, income, employment, inflation and productivity
  - Living standards, inequality and economic fluctuations

2. **Explain economic mechanisms and causal relationships**
  - Why do some countries grow faster than others?
  - What causes recessions, inflation and financial crises?

3. **Evaluate policies and institutions**
  - Monetary policy, fiscal policy and policies for long-run growth

4. **Forecast economic developments**

### What this course emphasizes

- Understanding and interpreting macroeconomic data

- Using deliberately simplified models to identify economic
          mechanisms and causal relationships

- Analyzing long-run growth, short-run fluctuations and the
          international economy

- Evaluating monetary policy, fiscal policy and economic
          institutions

- Connecting the models to historical and current events, with a
          global perspective and particular attention to Sweden

## Models and data

### Why models?

- Many macroeconomic questions are causal or counterfactual:
  - What causes economic growth?
  - What would happen if the central bank lowered the interest rate?
  - How would higher government spending affect output and inflation?

- A model makes assumptions explicit and isolates particular
          economic mechanisms

- It distinguishes between:
  - **exogenous variables**, which are taken as given
  - **endogenous variables**, which are determined within the model

- Once equilibrium has been characterized, we can change an
          exogenous variable and trace the effects on endogenous outcomes

### Different questions require different models

- All models simplify reality: what should be included depends on
          the question being studied

- A model of long-run growth emphasizes production, capital
          accumulation, technology and institutions

- A model of short-run fluctuations emphasizes aggregate demand,
          monetary policy, price adjustment and expectations

- A more complicated model is not necessarily a better model

``The art of successful theorizing is to make the inevitable simplifying
assumptions in such a way that the final results are not very sensitive.''

 — Robert Solow

### Models and data work together

**Data help us**
- measure economic outcomes
- identify regularities
- estimate causal effects
- assess competing explanations

    **Models help us**
- organize economic mechanisms
- interpret the evidence
- connect causes and effects
- analyze counterfactual policies

Throughout the course, we will move back and forth between economic data,
historical and current events and deliberately simplified models.
