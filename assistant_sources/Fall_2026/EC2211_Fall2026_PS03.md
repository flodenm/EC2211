## 1. Consumption and leisure

Consider a household that has $N$ hours per month to allocate between work and leisure. Let $L$ denote the number of hours chosen for leisure, let $C$ denote the household’s consumption and let $w$ denote the hourly wage rate. The household cannot save or borrow. The household’s utility each month is $U(C,L)=\ln{C} + \ln{L}$.

- **a.** Write down the household’s budget constraint.

- **b.** Write down the household’s optimization problem.

- **c.** Solve the household’s optimization problem.

- **d.** How are these choices affected by an increase in the wage rate? Explain the economic intuition.

## 2. Flows on the labor market

Consider an economy with 200 people of working age. Of these, 100 have a strong attachment to the labor market and are always employed. The remaining 100 move between employment, unemployment and being outside the labor force.

Among the employed with weak attachment, 10 percent lose their job in each period. Among the unemployed, 30 percent find a job while 10 percent become discouraged and leave the labor force. Of the people outside the labor force, in each period 10 percent are activated and enter the labor force as unemployed.

- **a.** What equations characterize the steady state in this economy?

- **b.** In steady state, how many persons are employed, unemployed and outside the labor force, respectively? What are the employment and unemployment rates?

- **c.** Suppose that the government launches a new policy that makes it more attractive to participate in the labor force and actively search for a job. As a result, the activation rate increases to 60 percent. What is then the new steady state? In particular, what are the new employment and unemployment rates? Explain why the employment and unemployment rates change in these directions.

## 3. Consumption and saving

Consider a household that lives for two periods. It has no initial wealth but receives income $Y_1>0$ in the first period. This income can either be consumed ($C_1$) or saved for the second period ($S\geq 0$). The interest rate is $r$, so saving $S$ provides $(1+r)S$ in the second period. The household has no other income in the second period and consumes all its accumulated saving. Its lifetime utility is 

$$
U(C_1,C_2)=\ln C_1+\beta\ln C_2,
    \qquad 0<\beta\leq 1.
$$

- **a.** Write down the household’s two per-period budget constraints and combine them into an intertemporal budget constraint.

- **b.** Write down the household’s optimization problem.

- **c.** Solve for optimal $C_1$, $C_2$ and $S$.

- **d.** How are $C_1$, $C_2$ and $S$ affected by an increase in the interest rate? Explain why.

## 4. Analyzing macroeconomic events with the IS curve

(Exercise 3 in Jones chapter 11) Consider the following changes in the macroeconomy. Show how to think about them using the IS curve, and explain how and why \[U.S.\] GDP is affected in the short run.

- **a.** The government offers a temporary investment tax credit: for each dollar of investment that firms undertake, they receive a credit that reduces the taxes they pay on corporate income.

- **b.** A booming economy in Europe this year leads to an unexpected increase in the demand by European consumers for U.S. goods.

- **c.** U.S. consumers develop an infatuation with all things made in New Zealand and sharply increase their imports from that country.

- **d.** A housing bubble bursts so that housing prices fall by 20% and new home sales drop sharply.
