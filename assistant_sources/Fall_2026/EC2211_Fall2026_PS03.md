## Consumption and leisure

Consider a household that has $N$ hours per month to allocate between work and leisure. Let $L$ denote the number of hours chosen for leisure, let $C$ denote the household's consumption and let $w$ denote the hourly wage rate. The household cannot save or borrow. The household's utility each month is $U(C,L)=\ln{C} + \ln{L}$.
a. Write down the household's budget constraint.
b. Write down the household's optimization problem.
c. Solve the household's optimization problem.
d. How are these choices affected by an increase in the wage rate?
        Explain the economic intuition.

## Consumption and saving

Consider a household that lives for two periods. It has no initial wealth but
receives income $Y_1>0$ in the first period. This income can either be consumed
($C_1$) or saved for the second period ($S\geq 0$). The interest rate is $r$,
so saving $S$ provides $(1+r)S$ in the second period. The household has no
other income in the second period and consumes all its accumulated saving.
Its lifetime utility is

$$U(C_1,C_2)=\ln C_1+\beta\ln C_2,
      0<\beta\leq 1.$$

a. Write down the household's two per-period budget constraints and
        combine them into an intertemporal budget constraint.
b. Write down the household's optimization problem.
c. Solve for optimal $C_1$, $C_2$ and $S$.
d. How are $C_1$, $C_2$ and $S$ affected by an increase in the
        interest rate? Explain why.

## Analyzing macroeconomic events with the IS curve

(Exercise 3 in Jones chapter 11) Consider the following changes in the macroeconomy. Show how to think about them using the IS curve, and explain how and why [U.S.] GDP is affected in the short run.
a. The government offers a temporary investment tax credit: for each dollar of investment that firms undertake, they receive a credit that reduces the taxes they pay on corporate income.
b. A booming economy in Europe this year leads to an unexpected increase in the demand by European consumers for U.S. goods.
c. U.S. consumers develop an infatuation with all things made in New Zealand and sharply increase their imports from that country.
d. A housing bubble bursts so that housing prices fall by 20% and new home sales drop sharply.

## A consumption boom

(Exercises 3 and 10 in Jones chapter 12) Using the IS/MP diagram, explain what happens to the economy if there is a temporary consumption boom that lasts for one period.
a. Initially, suppose that the central bank keeps the nominal interest rate unchanged.
b. Suppose you are appointed to chair the Federal Reserve. What monetary policy action would you take in this case and why? Refer to the IS/MP diagram.
c. Now explain what happens in the full short-run model (including the Phillips curve and allowing the economy to evolve over time). Do this for both parts (a) and (b), and be sure to provide graphs of output and inflation over time.
